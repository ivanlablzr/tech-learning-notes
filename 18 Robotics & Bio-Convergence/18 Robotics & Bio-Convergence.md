> [!info] Domain note — the machine ↔ organism frontier. Where engineering and biology stop being separate.

## Overview

Humanity is pursuing two problems that look alike but aren't: **(1) build machines that behave like living things** (robotics), and **(2) repair, replace or augment living bodies** (prosthetics, bio-convergence). The technologies overlap heavily — actuators, sensors, neural control, materials — which is why they belong in one domain.

> [!question] The five questions
> **Why it exists:** to engineer systems where mechanics, electronics, materials, control, AI and *living tissue* work together.
> **Problem it solves:** machines that perceive/act in the messy physical world, and engineered replacements/augmentations for the body.
> **Depends on:** [[02 Electronics]], [[03 Computer Hardware]] (compute/sensors), [[14 AI]] (perception/control), [[01 Physics]]/[[00 Mathematics]] (mechanics, control theory) — plus **biology, neuroscience, materials science** (your Knowledge Library: Neuroscience, Biology, Chemistry).
> **Depended on by:** future medicine, manufacturing, human augmentation, and humanoid robotics.
> **Interactions:** sensors → compute → AI control → actuators is the same loop as a [[Hardware Interaction & Data Flow|nervous system → muscle]] loop; this domain makes that analogy literal.

### The two sub-fields
- **[[Robotics & Biomechatronics]]** — building bodies: structure, actuators (incl. **artificial muscles**), sensing, control, humanoids, prosthetics, neural interfaces, soft robotics, biomechanics.
- **[[Bio-Convergence Technologies]]** — merging biology + engineering: brain–computer interfaces, organoid intelligence, electronic skin, microrobotic medicine, DNA nanotech, bioprinting, programmable cells, xenobots.

## Learning Path
The deepest path runs bottom-up through the dependency stack (see [[Master Index — Technology Vault]]):
1. [[00 Mathematics|Calculus]] → 2. [[01 Physics]] (mechanics, EM) → 3. Mechanics → 4. Materials science → 5. [[02 Electronics]] → 6. **Control theory** → 7. Robotics → 8. Biomechanics → 9. Neuroscience → 10. Biomedical engineering → 11. [[14 AI|Machine Learning]].
*That progression takes you from "why a motor turns" → "how a robot walks" → "how a prosthetic is driven by a nervous system."*
