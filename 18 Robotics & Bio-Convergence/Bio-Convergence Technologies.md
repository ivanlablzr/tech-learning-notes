> [[18 Robotics & Bio-Convergence]] → fields where **biology and engineering merge**. Under-reported vs AI because they're highly specialized, often pre-clinical, ethically/regulatorily constrained, and don't generate easy headlines — yet many could reshape medicine and human-machine interaction as profoundly as the internet reshaped communication.

# Bio-Convergence Technologies

The unifying theme: **the boundary between living tissue and technology is becoming permeable.** Instead of building machines *and separately* treating bodies, engineers design systems where tissue, electronics, materials and computation work *together*.

## Computation that uses biology
- **Organoid intelligence / biological computing** — living neurons (rat, human stem-cell-derived, or **brain organoids**) grown on **microelectrode arrays**, receiving signals, learning patterns, producing outputs. Explored for energy efficiency and adaptive learning that silicon ([[03 Computer Hardware]], [[14 AI]]) lacks.
- **Brain organoids** — tiny stem-cell-grown brain-like structures (neurons + synapses + activity; *not* mini-brains) used to study autism, Alzheimer's, development, drug effects.
- **DNA data storage** — encode images/video/databases into synthetic DNA; extraordinary density and millennia-long stability (limits: cost, read/write speed).
- **DNA nanotechnology** — DNA as a *construction material*: self-assembling cages/boxes/machines that carry and release molecular payloads.
- **Programmable cells (synthetic biology)** — cells engineered as biological computers: `if cancer marker detected → release therapy`.

## Interfacing with the nervous system
- **Brain–Computer Interfaces (BCI)** — decode movement intent, restore communication, drive robotic limbs/cursors. Paralysed patients already type and control arms via neural signals (Neuralink, BrainGate). Core to [[Robotics & Biomechatronics|neural-controlled prosthetics]].
- **Optogenetics** — genetically modify neurons to respond to light → activate/deactivate specific neurons with extreme precision (transformative neuroscience tool; connects to your Knowledge Library *Neuroscience* notes).
- **Neural dust** — proposed microscopic, wirelessly-powered implanted sensors for distributed body-wide sensing (largely experimental).

## Bionic body parts & materials
- **Electronic skin** — flexible sensors for pressure/temperature/strain → prosthetics, humanoids, wearables; goal: `artificial hand + artificial skin + neural interface = real touch`.
- **Soft implantable / injectable electronics** — bend/stretch/conform to tissue (vs rigid implants) → less inflammation, better long-term compatibility; some can be injected rather than surgically implanted.
- **Artificial retinas** — `camera → processor → retina implant → vision` for some forms of blindness.
- **Artificial blood vessels** — tissue-engineered grafts / biodegradable scaffolds that integrate into the body.

## Medicine at the micro scale
- **Magnetic microrobots & micro-swimmers** — externally steered (magnetic fields) devices inspired by bacteria/flagella for **targeted drug delivery, clot removal**.
- **Smart / targeted drug delivery** — nanoparticles that accumulate in tumours/inflamed tissue, cutting side effects (`drug → specific tissue` vs `drug → whole body`).
- **Closed-loop medical devices** — `measure → decide → respond` (e.g. **artificial pancreas**: sense glucose → compute → deliver insulin).

## Engineered & hybrid living systems
- **Lab-grown mini-organs (organoids)** — liver/kidney/lung/intestine for drug testing & disease modelling (reduce animal testing).
- **Bioprinting** — 3D printing with living cells (skin, cartilage, vessels; goal: printable organs).
- **Biohybrid robots** — living muscle-powered actuators, jellyfish/insect cyborgs (locomotion research).
- **Xenobots** — assembled from frog cells; move, respond, perform simple tasks — neither conventional robot nor organism.

## Fields to watch (20–40 year horizon)
BCIs · organoid intelligence · programmable cells · DNA nanotechnology · neural dust · artificial muscles · biohybrid robotics · targeted microrobotic medicine · regenerative medicine · electronic skin.
